# M18 — Content-Driven Timing & Audio Synchronization

M18 changes the timing philosophy of the video engine.

## No fixed video duration

There is no target such as “60 seconds per concept” and no hard maximum scene duration.

A scene lasts as long as is required for:
- accurate explanation
- understandable pacing
- visual demonstration
- mathematical derivation
- examples
- comprehension pauses
- question response time

## Audio is authoritative

The narration/voice track determines the actual scene duration.

Initial planning may estimate duration from word count, but after TTS or recorded audio is produced, measured audio duration replaces the estimate.

Pipeline:

Scene plan
→ narration
→ TTS/recording
→ actual audio timing
→ visual retiming
→ Remotion render

## Word-level synchronization

Production TTS/ASR should provide word-level timestamps.
These timestamps become sync markers that can trigger:
- highlighting
- equation reveals
- diagram movement
- camera changes
- labels
- emphasis
- transitions

## Presentation planner

The system chooses actions according to the pedagogical role.

Example derivation:
state target → variables → stepwise derivation → verification.

Example process:
orient → step-by-step animation → relationships → comprehension pause.

Example checkpoint:
question → response pause → explanation/reveal.

## Critical rule

Animation must follow explanation, not the other way around.

If a derivation needs 2 minutes, the scene may be 2 minutes.
If a simple definition needs 15 seconds, it may be 15 seconds.
No artificial duration compression is allowed merely to meet a preset video length.
