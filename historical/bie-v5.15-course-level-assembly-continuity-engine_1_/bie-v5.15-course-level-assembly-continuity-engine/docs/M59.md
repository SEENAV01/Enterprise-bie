# M59 — Course-Level Assembly & Continuity Engine

M59 is the top-level assembly layer for long-form educational content.

## Core purpose

A course must feel like one coherent work rather than a collection of independently generated lessons.

M59 manages:
- course structure
- chapter structure
- lesson order
- global timeline
- cross-lesson continuity
- recurring visual motifs
- chapter openings and closings
- cumulative duration
- final-course assembly metadata

## Hierarchy

`Course → Chapter → Lesson → Scene`

Every level has an explicit identity and order.

## Global timeline

Lesson durations come from their content-driven timelines.

M59 assigns each lesson an absolute course-level offset.

There is no requirement that lessons have equal or fixed durations.

## Continuity

A global continuity contract can define:
- style identity
- registered assets
- recurring motifs
- motion language

Lessons are checked against this contract.

Silent drift can therefore be detected.

## Recurring motifs

Motifs are semantic recurring elements, not random decoration.

Examples:
- a recurring chapter marker
- a consistent concept icon
- a visual metaphor reused throughout the course
- a recurring character/object

Each appearance is registered.

## Chapter openings and closings

Chapters can have explicit:
- openings
- objective previews
- summaries
- next-section previews

Their duration remains content-driven.

## Assembly

The final manifest contains:
- course identity
- chapter order
- lesson order
- lesson absolute timing
- cumulative course duration
- final assembly status

## Relationship to previous modules

M54 established visual style and asset continuity.

M55 established shot planning.

M58 established the master lesson timeline.

M59 extends that timeline and continuity model across the complete course.

## Critical distinction

M59 assembles and maintains continuity.

It does not replace the pedagogical planner, domain reasoning engine, visual planner or renderer.
