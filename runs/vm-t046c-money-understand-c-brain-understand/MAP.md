## Concepts

### Money: meaning, forms and characteristics · definition

Book: Money is anything accepted for exchanging goods and services and settling debts, including cash, bank deposits and digital balances rather than notes and coins alone. Good money combines acceptability, durability, portability, divisibility, uniformity and limited supply; the book links scarcity with preservation of value. Outside clarification: uniformity means equivalent monetary units have equal value, not that different denominations have identical value.

### Barter's limitations and the four functions of money · cause_effect

Book: Barter directly exchanges goods or services without money at mutually agreed values; common in early civilizations and still present in limited informal exchanges, it faces double coincidence of wants, no common value measure, indivisibility, difficult wealth storage and difficult deferred payment, while money functions as medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and standard of deferred payment. Outside explanation: an accepted medium removes the need to find someone who both wants your goods and offers yours; a common unit replaces numerous goods-to-goods valuations, divisible monetary units permit small payments without cutting an indivisible good, stored money carries purchasing power forward, and money-denominated debts specify future obligations without deciding which goods or qualities must later be delivered. Book examples: ₹500 buys groceries instead of exchanging wheat for rice; a ₹20,000 phone and ₹60,000 laptop use a common price unit; bank savings fund later expenses; home loans and EMIs specify deferred payments.

### Intrinsic versus extrinsic value · comparison

Book: Intrinsic-value money draws worth from its material and retains non-monetary value, illustrated by gold, silver, salt, cattle, cowrie shells, metal coins and bullion; extrinsic-value money draws monetary worth from trust and authority, illustrated by notes, bank balances, digital money and UPI payments. The table contrasts utility- and availability-based acceptance with legal backing and trust, generally durable commodities with variable paper/technology durability, and difficult commodity division and transport with easier modern payments. Outside clarification: 'nil value' should be understood as negligible material value relative to monetary value, and UPI is a payment interface rather than a separate stock of money.

### The schematic evolution of money · timeline

Book: The six illustrated stages run left to right: barter → commodity money → metallic money → paper money → bank money → digital money. ### Commodity money: uses, strengths and limitations · classification

Book: Commodity money has value outside monetary exchange; community acceptance makes it more useful than barter and removes double coincidence of wants. ### Bretton Woods, Triffin's dilemma and the Nixon decision · cause_effect

Book: Under Bretton Woods the US promised, mainly to foreign central banks, conversion of US$35 into one troy ounce of gold; the diagram places gold above the reserve dollar and other currencies below it. ### The Indian rupee: silver origins to colonial unification · timeline

Book: Rupiya derives from Sanskrit Rupya, wrought silver; Sher Shah Suri's 1540 silver Rupiya weighed about 178 grains/11.5 grams and was high-purity and uniform, while Mughal standardization during 1556–1707 expanded mints, common weights and imperial names. Match Mohur–gold, Rupee–silver and Dam–copper; during 1757–1835 the East India Company initially retained Mughal-style coinage while Bombay, Calcutta and Madras minted differing coins. The Currency Act, 1835 established a uniform British Indian rupee and common legal tender, and the Paper Currency Act, 1861 gave government the exclusive banknote-issue right.

### The rupee: gold exchange, decimalization and managed float · timeline

Book: Large silver discoveries and falling silver prices frame the 1873–1947 discussion, followed by the Fowler Committee's 1898 gold-link recommendation; outside explanation: a Gold Exchange Standard links domestic money indirectly to gold through reserves and exchange arrangements in a gold-linked foreign currency, rather than promising domestic holders direct gold redemption. Book claims RBI began issuing notes in 1935 bearing George V and later George VI; outside correction: RBI was established in 1935 and first issued banknotes in 1938 bearing George VI, not George V. ### Gresham's Law and the incentive to hoard good money · cause_effect

Book: 'Bad money drives out good money' when both are legal tender at government-fixed exchange values, one is undervalued relative to intrinsic worth and people can choose which to spend. With equal face values, higher-intrinsic-value good money is hoarded or melted while lower-intrinsic-value bad money is spent and remains in everyday circulation. Henry VIII's sixteenth-century English silver debasement illustrates purer older coins being retained while debased coins circulated.

### Money supply, reserve money and bank expansion · cause_effect

### M3 and M4: broad money, policy relevance and exclusions · comparison

Book: M3 = M1 + bank time deposits, including fixed, recurring and other commercial-bank time deposits; it is the principal Indian money-supply measure used in reporting and watched for inflationary pressure, liquidity, credit growth and monetary-policy effectiveness.
