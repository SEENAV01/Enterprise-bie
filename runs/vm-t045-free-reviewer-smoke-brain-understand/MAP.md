## Concepts

### Tissue and its plant–animal grouping · definition

Book: A tissue is formed when cells combine to perform the same function; plant cells form plant tissues and animal cells form animal tissues. The defining idea is coordinated function, while plant versus animal identifies the biological group to which the cells belong.

Items: p001-i3, p001-i4, p001-i5

### Division of labour and reduced cellular workload · cause_effect

Book: Tissue formation causes division of labour, decreases the workload on individual cells and leads to organisation. Outside explanation: Specialised groups share different tasks, so an individual cell need not perform the full range of functions required by a multicellular organism.

Items: p001-i6, p001-i7

### Levels of biological organisation · structure

Book: The arrangement is Cell → Tissues → Organ → Organ system → Organisms, placing tissues between cells and organs and organ systems between organs and organisms. Outside explanation: Tissues contribute to organs, and cooperating organs form organ systems; these are nested structural levels, not stages in which one cell directly changes into an organism.

Items: p001-i9, p001-i10, p001-i11, p001-i12, p001-i13

### Mitosis versus meiosis: occurrence, products and roles · comparison

Book: Mitosis occurs in somatic cells, forms two diploid daughter cells, occurs more frequently and produces daughter cells that form somatic organs; meiosis occurs in germ cells, forms four haploid daughter cells, occurs less frequently and produces cells associated with gamete formation. Outside qualification: This is the book's simplified comparison; mitosis preserves the starting chromosome-set number rather than necessarily producing diploid cells, and meiotic products do not directly become gametes in every life cycle.

Items: p001-i15, p001-i17, p001-i18, p001-i19, p001-i20

### Mitosis versus meiosis: nuclear divisions and phases · comparison

Book: Mitosis has one nuclear division with one prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase; meiosis has two nuclear divisions and two occurrences of these phases. Prophase I of meiosis has five sub-phases, so its distinctive detail belongs to the first meiotic division rather than to both divisions.

Items: p001-i16, p001-i21

### Chromosome-number outcomes and crossing over · comparison

Book: Mitosis maintains daughter-cell chromosome number and is equational division; meiosis halves chromosome number and is reduction division, with crossing over contrasted against its absence in mitosis. Outside explanation: Homologous chromosomes separate in meiosis I, producing reduction, while sister chromatids separate in meiosis II; meiotic crossing over exchanges segments between nonsister chromatids of homologous chromosomes.

Items: p001-i22, p001-i24, p001-i25

### DNA replication is not chromosome-number doubling · comparison

Book claim, scientifically incorrect: The row says chromosome number doubles at the beginning of mitosis, does not double at the beginning of meiosis and doubles after the first meiotic division. Outside correction, also flagged in the supplied transcription: DNA replicates before mitosis and before meiosis I, with no DNA replication between meiosis I and II. Replication creates sister chromatids and increases DNA content; it does not itself double chromosome number.

Items: p001-i23

### Plant versus animal mitosis · comparison

Book: Plant mitosis is described as lacking centrioles and asters, involving cell-plate formation and occurring mainly at meristems; animal mitosis has centrioles and asters, involves furrowing and cleavage of cytoplasm, and occurs in tissues throughout the body. Outside explanation and qualification: A cell plate partitions the plant cell, whereas a cleavage furrow constricts the animal cell; the table presents a standard contrast, not a claim that every plant or animal cell follows it without exception.

Items: p001-i27, p001-i28, p001-i29, p001-i30

### Main classification of plant tissues · classification

Book: Plant tissue branches into Meristematic Tissue and Permanent Tissue. Outside explanation: This distinction separates actively dividing growth tissue from differentiated tissue whose cells generally no longer divide, providing a meaningful basis for the two branches.

Items: p001-i32

### Meristematic tissue and its three types · classification

Book: Meristematic Tissue divides into Apical Meristem, Lateral Meristem and Intercalary Meristem. Outside explanation: Apical meristems occur at root and shoot tips and support length growth; lateral meristems support increased girth, while intercalary meristems occur between mature tissues, for example at leaf bases or internode bases in grasses.

Items: p001-i33, p001-i34, p001-i35, p001-i36

### Permanent tissue: simple and complex · classification

Book: Permanent Tissue branches into Simple and Complex; Simple leads to parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma, while Complex leads to xylem and phloem. Outside explanation: Simple tissues consist of one principal cell type, whereas complex tissues contain different cell types working together.

Items: p001-i37, p001-i38, p001-i39

### Simple permanent tissues and sclerenchyma subdivisions · classification

Book: Parenchyma, printed as 'Paranchyma', Collenchyma and Sclerenchyma are simple permanent tissues; Fibres and Sclereids are subdivisions of Sclerenchyma, not additional peers of the three simple tissues. Outside explanation: Parenchyma commonly contains living, relatively thin-walled cells; collenchyma provides flexible support through uneven wall thickening, while sclerenchyma provides strength through thick, lignified walls, with fibres generally elongated and sclereids shorter and variable in shape.

Items: p001-i40, p001-i41, p001-i42, p001-i43, p001-i44

### Xylem and its visible components · classification

Book: Xylem belongs to complex permanent tissue, and its visible component list contains Tracheids, Vessels (Tracheae) and Xylem parenchyma; cropping prevents treating this list as complete. Outside explanation: Xylem transports water and dissolved minerals; tracheids and vessels are conducting elements, whereas xylem parenchyma consists of living cells contributing to storage and lateral transport.

Items: p001-i45, p001-i46, p001-i47, p001-i48

### Phloem and its visible components · classification

Book: Phloem belongs to complex permanent tissue, and the visible components are Sieve tubes, Companion cells and Phloem parenchyma; the cropped diagram does not establish an exhaustive list. Outside explanation: Phloem transports organic food materials; sieve-tube elements conduct them, companion cells support sieve-tube functioning, and phloem parenchyma contributes to storage and lateral movement.

Items: p001-i49, p001-i50, p001-i51, p001-i52
