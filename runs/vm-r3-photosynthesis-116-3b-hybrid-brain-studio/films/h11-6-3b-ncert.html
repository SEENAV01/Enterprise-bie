141Why are we so interested in the proton gradient?✓
  • Difference ka fayda Ab socho, proton gradient mein itni interest kyun hai; sirf lumen mein H⁺ jama karna final goal hai?
141This gradient is important because it is the breakdown of this gradient that leads to the synthesis of ATP.✓
  • Difference ka fayda Iska seedha sa jawab hai, lumen ke H⁺ ka ATP synthase se stroma ki taraf controlled return gradient ki stored energy ko ATP synthesis se couple karta hai.
  • Breakdown ka matlab Breakdown of this gradient ka matlab dono taraf ka difference kam hona hai, H⁺ ka destroy hona nahi.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Ab poora mechanism teen stages mein phir jodo: gradient banao, H⁺ ka controlled return karao, aur ATP synthesis se energy capture karo.
141The gradient is broken down due to the movement of protons across the membrane to the stroma through the transmembrane channel of the CF₀ of the ATP synthase.✓
  • Difference ka fayda Iska seedha sa jawab hai, lumen ke H⁺ ka ATP synthase se stroma ki taraf controlled return gradient ki stored energy ko ATP synthesis se couple karta hai.
  • Breakdown ka matlab Return route transmembrane hai, yaani thylakoid membrane ke aar-paar phaila hai.
  • Downhill return H⁺ CF₀ se lumen se stroma ko facilitated diffusion karte hain, yaani protein route se apne gradient ki direction mein cross karte hain.
  • Shape change se ATP Ek H⁺ ko follow karo: woh lumen se CF₀ cross karke stroma tak aata hai, aur coupled enzyme changes ATP production support karte hain.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Phir H⁺ lumen se CF₀ ke through stroma lautte hain, aur CF₁ ke conformational changes ATP formation support karte hain.
142The ATP synthase enzyme consists of two parts: one called the CF₀ is embedded in the thylakoid membrane and forms a transmembrane channel that carries out facilitated diffusion of protons across the membrane.✓
  • Do main parts Ab return route ko enlarge karo: ATP synthase ke do main parts CF₀ aur CF₁ hain.
  • Do main parts CF₀ thylakoid membrane mein embedded proton channel hai; zero subscript wala yeh part membrane ko span karta hai.
  • Downhill return H⁺ CF₀ se lumen se stroma ko facilitated diffusion karte hain, yaani protein route se apne gradient ki direction mein cross karte hain.
142The other portion is called CF₁ and protrudes on the outer surface of the thylakoid membrane on the side that faces the stroma.✓
  • Do main parts Ab return route ko enlarge karo: ATP synthase ke do main parts CF₀ aur CF₁ hain.
  • Do main parts CF₁ stroma ki taraf nikla enzyme head hai, lumen ki taraf nahi.
  • Supplies ka agla kaam Dekho, ATP synthase ka working head stroma-facing hai aur sugar-making reactions stroma mein hoti hain, isliye membrane ki supplies wahin use hoti hain.
142The break down of the gradient provides enough energy to cause a conformational change in the CF₁ particle of the ATP synthase, which makes the enzyme synthesise several molecules of energy-packed ATP.✓
  • Shape change se ATP Proton return se released gradient energy CF₁ ki conformation badalne ke kaam aati hai.
  • Shape change se ATP Conformational change protein ki three-dimensional shape mein change hai.
  • Shape change se ATP In changes se ATP synthase ADP aur inorganic phosphate, yaani Pi, se ATP synthesis kar paata hai.
  • Shape change se ATP Aisa isliye hai kyunki ATP synthase repeated proton flow se kaam karta hai; single proton aur single ATP ko one-to-one token pair nahi banana chahiye.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Phir H⁺ lumen se CF₀ ke through stroma lautte hain, aur CF₁ ke conformational changes ATP formation support karte hain.
142Chemiosmosis requires a membrane, a proton pump, a proton gradient and ATP synthase.✓
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Chemiosmosis ki chaar requirements hain: membrane, proton pump, proton gradient aur ATP synthase.
142Energy is used to pump protons across a membrane, to create a gradient or a high concentration of protons within the thylakoid lumen.✓
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Proton pump energy use karke H⁺ ko membrane ke across accumulate karane wala transport system hai.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Thylakoid lumen wahi enclosed inside space hai; water oxidation aur carrier-linked transfer wahaan H⁺ badhaate hain, jabki NADP⁺ reduction stromal H⁺ ghataati hai.
142ATP synthase has a channel that allows diffusion of protons back across the membrane; this releases enough energy to activate ATP synthase enzyme that catalyses the formation of ATP.✓
  • Downhill return Hota yeh hai ki gradient pehle stored energy rakhta hai, isliye return downhill hai; pumping ne difference banaya tha, diffusion us difference ko use karti hai.
  • Shape change se ATP In changes se ATP synthase ADP aur inorganic phosphate, yaani Pi, se ATP synthesis kar paata hai.
  • Shape change se ATP Ek H⁺ ko follow karo: woh lumen se CF₀ cross karke stroma tak aata hai, aur coupled enzyme changes ATP production support karte hain.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Ab poora mechanism teen stages mein phir jodo: gradient banao, H⁺ ka controlled return karao, aur ATP synthesis se energy capture karo.
  • Chemiosmosis ko jodo Phir H⁺ lumen se CF₀ ke through stroma lautte hain, aur CF₁ ke conformational changes ATP formation support karte hain.
142Along with the NADPH produced by the movement of electrons, the ATP will be used immediately in the biosynthetic reaction taking place in the stroma, responsible for fixing CO₂, and synthesis of sugars.✓
  • Stroma ko supplies Ab light-driven electron movement se mila NADPH aur chemiosmosis se bana ATP stroma ki biosynthetic reactions ko supply hote hain.
  • Stroma ko supplies ATP usable energy deta hai, aur NADPH reducing power deta hai; unke complementary roles hum pehle pehchaan chuke hain.
  • Stroma ko supplies Fixing CO₂ ka matlab CO₂ ke carbon ko organic molecule mein jodna hai.
  • Supplies ka agla kaam Dekho, ATP synthase ka working head stroma-facing hai aur sugar-making reactions stroma mein hoti hain, isliye membrane ki supplies wahin use hoti hain.
  • Supplies ka agla kaam Light reaction ne ab energy aur reducing power taiyaar ki; carbon se sugars banne ka detailed raasta aage kholenge.
